## Phase 1 Conclusion

The project should now be described as:

> A reviewable artificial-organization research method for studying institutional friction, evidence gaps, and non-intentional control-slippage boundaries through LLM-controlled roles, deterministic Game Master decisions, evidence packs, validators, candidate review, and explicit claim boundaries.

The project should not be described as:

> A reproduction of human social chaos or proof of real organizational failure.

Phase 1 therefore changes the project emphasis from "can we reproduce social chaos?" to "can we build a disciplined artificial evidence method that separates what happened, what was reviewed, what remains unsupported, and what can become a future hypothesis?"

## What Has Been Learned

### About The Research Method

The project has built a staged artifact pipeline:

- governance and claim rules;
- artificial-world protocols;
- role/action and Game Master contracts;
- evidence-pack generation and validation;
- LLM and non-LLM execution paths;
- human review for curated EXP-0002 representative packs;
- construct-validity review;
- Method B and Method B+ candidate review;
- synthesis and claim-boundary documents.

This supports a strong artifact-method claim, not an external social claim.

### About Institutional Friction

The project has bounded artificial evidence for:

- evidence gaps;
- approval-evidence propagation;
- coordination holds;
- pressure context;
- descriptive scenario contrasts;
- limited provider-randomness sensitivity;
- one adjacent-domain transfer pilot.

These are artificial-system observations and reviewable hypotheses for future work. They are not human or real-world findings.

### About Non-Intentional Control Slippage

Method B+ refined the control-slippage model:

- SL2 buyer-side payment-forward handoff can appear under bounded artificial conditions, especially lossy handoff.
- SL5 downstream evidence-gap preservation appears repeatedly in S17, S18, and S19.
- SL3 accountant payment preparation without explicit approval is not supported.
- SL4 final payment-ready state without explicit approval is not supported.
- SL6 evidence-gap erasure is not supported.
- FM1 responsibility diffusion, FM3 ambiguous-guidance misinterpretation, and FM6 post-hoc justification remain unsupported, reviewed rejected, or not observed.

The useful result is stage separation. The project can now avoid treating "approval bypass" as one undifferentiated label.

## Current Research Questions

Phase 1 accepts the updated research questions from `docs/research/research-questions-v0.2.md`:

1. How can artificial organizations be structured so role actions, Game Master decisions, traces, events, metrics, and final states are reconstructable?
2. How can generated candidates, reviewed support, partial support, rejected candidates, not-observed outcomes, and unsupported claims be kept separate?
3. Which institutional-friction-like patterns can be represented and reviewed in artificial org-payment settings?
4. At which stage does a process move forward, and at which stage is the approval or evidence gap preserved?
5. Under current artificial protocols, what conditions appear to preserve control boundaries rather than produce stronger slippage?
6. What would count as a genuinely different mechanism if future work continues?

## Current Claim Boundary

The project may claim:

- a versioned artificial-organization research pipeline exists;
- evidence packs and validators make many artifacts mechanically reviewable;
- selected EXP-0002 representative packs have bounded human-review support;
- some constructs are usable with construct-validity limits;
- Method B+ supports narrow SL2 buyer handoff and repeated SL5 downstream preservation under artificial conditions;
- the artifact pipeline can generate hypotheses for future institutional-friction research.

The project may not claim:

- LLM agents reproduce human society;
- real organizations behave like these runs;
- full approval bypass has been reproduced;
- SL3, SL4, or SL6 is supported;
- responsibility diffusion, ambiguous-guidance misinterpretation, or post-hoc justification has been proven;
- scenario differences are causal effects;
- results are statistically meaningful;
- the model is generally safe, unsafe, reliable, or unreliable;
- the artifacts provide compliance, legal, audit, operational, governance, or safety sufficiency.

## Phase 2 Readiness

Phase 2 should treat the current project as a methodology research contribution.

The next work should not chase a stronger failure result. It should document:

- the methodological contribution;
- the evidence-pack and review protocol;
- how negative and conservative results are preserved;
- why protocol freeze, evidence reconstruction, candidate review, and claim boundaries are the main contribution even when stronger failure modes are unsupported.
